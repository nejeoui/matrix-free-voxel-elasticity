\section{Algebra, quadrature and counter
methods}\label{algebra-quadrature-and-counter-methods}

This supplement specifies the operator construction and measurement

\subsection{Explicit parity permutation and coefficient
convention}\label{explicit-parity-permutation-and-coefficient-convention}

Let the native Hex8 node order be
\texttt{\seqsplit{(000,100,110,010,001,101,111,011)}}, and encode a
binary node as \texttt{n\ =\ nx\ +\ 2\ ny\ +\ 4\ nz}. Let
\texttt{p=0,...,7} label coordinate modes and \texttt{c=0,1,2} label the
x, y and z displacement components. Define

\[
H_{pn}=(-1)^{\operatorname{popcount}(p\mathbin{\&}n)},\qquad
T_{3p+c,3i+d}=H_{p,n_i}\delta_{cd},\qquad TT^\top=8I.
\]

Thus \texttt{T} includes the native-to-binary node-column permutation
and its output uses \texttt{(p,c)} order, with flat index \texttt{3p+c}.
The block order is \texttt{(g,c)}, with \texttt{g\ =\ p\ xor\ 2\^{}c}
and flat index \texttt{3g+c}. The permutation matrix is

\[
P_{3g+c,\;3(g\oplus2^c)+c}=1
\]

with all other entries zero. Write \texttt{A\ =\ T\ K0\ T\^{}T}. Each
block is completely specified by

\[
(C_g)_{cd}=A_{3(g\oplus2^c)+c,\;3(g\oplus2^d)+d},
\quad c,d\in\{0,1,2\}.
\]

For the stated element class,

\[
C=PTK_0T^\top P^\top=\operatorname{diag}(C_0,\ldots,C_7),\qquad
K_0u=\frac1{64}T^\top P^\top CPTu.
\]

The factor \texttt{1/64} arises because \texttt{T\^{}(-1)=T\^{}T/8}
appears on both sides of the transformed matrix. The implementation
stores \texttt{C\_g/64}, not \texttt{C\_g}:
\texttt{\seqsplit{code/hex\_modal/modal.py}} first forms
\texttt{full=T@K0@T.T/64}, then selects
\texttt{\seqsplit{GROUPS[g,c]=3*(g} \seqsplit{xor} \seqsplit{(1<<c))+c}}.
Consequently its two unnormalised butterflies need no final division. In
the eight-lane CUDA implementation, a lane owns mode \texttt{p} for all
three components; the x/y/z coefficient blocks have group indices
\texttt{p\ xor\ 1}, \texttt{p\ xor\ 2} and \texttt{p\ xor\ 4}. The
required partner for component \texttt{d} in output component \texttt{c}
is mode \texttt{p\ xor\ 2\^{}c\ xor\ 2\^{}d}. This implements \texttt{P}
and \texttt{P\^{}T} through indexing/shuffles without materialising a
permutation array.

The exactness class is axis-aligned rectangular trilinear Hex8 elements,
with an isotropic elasticity tensor constant within each element.
Density multiplies the element tensor by a scalar modulus; it does not
alter this symmetry. Under reflection of coordinate \texttt{a}, the
scalar coordinate mode has character \texttt{(-1)\^{}p\_a}, while
component \texttt{c} receives the additional vector sign
\texttt{(-1)\^{}{[}a=c{]}}. The vector-mode character is therefore
\texttt{(-1)\^{}(p\_a+{[}a=c{]})}. The elastic energy bilinear form is
invariant under each reflection. For two modes with different
characters, applying that reflection changes the sign of their coupling
while preserving its value, so their coupling must vanish. Equality of
all three characters is precisely equality of \texttt{g=p\ xor\ 2\^{}c}.
Each of the eight characters has three component-mode pairs. All 24
modes are retained. This argument does not extend the exactness claim to
distorted or sheared cells, arbitrary rotations with untransformed
material/components, anisotropy or material variation within the cell.

The fresh check uses \texttt{E=1}, \texttt{nu=0.3}, exact \texttt{2×2×2}
Gauss integration and FP64. Reconstruction means
\texttt{\seqsplit{||K0-Kreconstructed||F/||K0||F}}; off-block means
\texttt{\seqsplit{||C-blockdiag(C)||F/||C||F}}. Eigenvalues with
absolute value at most
\texttt{1e-10\ \textbar{}\textbar{}K0\textbar{}\textbar{}2} are counted
as zero. The six analytic translations/rotations are separately checked
as a null basis, and 24 coordinate vectors plus 32 seeded normal vectors
check the implemented product.

\subsection{Exact counter quantities and profiling
boundary}\label{exact-counter-quantities-and-profiling-boundary}

Session J attempt 07 profiled the third launch after skipping two
matching launches for each of five FP64 kernels on one RTX 4090 VM and a
\texttt{128×64×64} grid (\texttt{524288} elements). This is one selected
launch per kernel; each selected launch was replayed in twelve profiler
passes, not twelve independent observations. The input vector is seeded
normal (\texttt{numpy.default\_rng(7)}), and element moduli are
\texttt{1e-9+(1-1e-9)*rho³} for seeded uniform \texttt{rho} in
\texttt{{[}0,1)}.

\begin{verbatim}
smsp__sass_thread_inst_executed_op_dadd_pred_on.sum

separate quantity. Launched threads, active lanes and element count are

\subsection{Post-hoc accounting and bounded
interpretation}\label{post-hoc-accounting-and-bounded-interpretation}

For kernel \texttt{k}, define \texttt{N\_k} as the count above and
\texttt{U\_k} as the active-cycle FP64 pipe fraction. The descriptive
ratio is \texttt{\seqsplit{(N\_k/U\_k)/(N\_muladd/U\_muladd)}}. Its
maximum relative discrepancy from the same-launch profiled-duration
ratio is \texttt{1.768\%} (approximately \texttt{1.8\%}). Because the
utilization denominator already contains active execution-cycle
information from that launch, dividing counts by utilization partly
recovers cycle accounting. This is a post-hoc consistency result, not an
independent prediction of runtime, a held-out validation or an
explanation of every timing component.

H15's utilization subcondition passes. Its static-count-ratio
subcondition fails: the dense8/mul-add executed ratio \texttt{2.5}
differs from \texttt{78/34} by \texttt{8.974\%}, beyond the declared 5\%
threshold. This failed declaration remains visible. The smaller
plain/mul-add discrepancy (\texttt{4.615\%}) does not rescue the joint
hypothesis.

High FP64 pipe activity, low DRAM throughput and fewer executed FP64
instructions support FP64 throughput as a dominant constraint for these
profiled workloads. They do not independently exclude cache behavior,
latency or atomic costs, nor establish a bottleneck on the unprofiled
A100, H100 or RTX 3090. Recommendations outside this RTX 4090 workload
must instead be bounded by their own measured product and solver
endpoints.

\subsection{Hardware basis and idealized traffic of the
roofline}\label{hardware-basis-and-idealized-traffic-of-the-roofline}

The original declared roofline used the A100 80 GB \textbf{SXM}
specification: 9.7 non-tensor FP64 TFLOP/s and 2039 GB/s. Its original
FP64 ridge \texttt{4.7572\ FLOP/B} is retained and labeled. For the
measured A100 80 GB \textbf{PCIe}, the SKU-specific calculation uses 9.7
FP64 TFLOP/s, 19.5 FP32 TFLOP/s and 1935 GB/s from
\href{https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/a100/}{NVIDIA's A100
specification table}. The corresponding ridges are
\texttt{5.0129\ FLOP/B} (FP64) and \texttt{10.0775\ FLOP/B} (FP32). The
ideal dense/parity ratio remains \texttt{1.0} in both precisions. The
added row is an added SKU calculation; it is not relabeled as a
prospective prediction. Both hardware bases and their status are emitted
by \href{../../analysis/roofline.py}{roofline.py} to
\href{../roofline.json}{roofline.json} and
\href{../roofline.md}{roofline.md}.

The 48 values per element count one gather of 24 displacement values and
one scatter of 24 results. This is idealized traffic accounting, not
measured DRAM bytes or a universal traffic lower bound. Shared-node
reuse and cache hits can reduce DRAM loads; atomic read/modify/write
transactions, modulus reads and constraint masks add traffic. The model
excludes these effects and cannot establish their measured share of
runtime.

\subsection{Constitutive and discretization

this scalar multiplies its reference stiffness. The parity exactness
claim does not cover distorted cells, anisotropic constitutive laws, or
a tensor varying within an element.

\subsection{Flexible CG and product
accounting}\label{flexible-cg-and-product-accounting}

The rediscretized hierarchy halves all three extents while
\texttt{nx\textgreater{}16} and every extent is even. It stops early if
another halving would be nonintegral: the largest q96x3 hierarchy ends
at 18×9×9, for example. Noncoarsest operators are matrix-free, with
averaged child moduli and
\texttt{\seqsplit{K0\_coarse=2\textasciicircum{}level} \seqsplit{K0\_fine}};
the coarsest constrained matrix is assembled and factored by dense
Cholesky.

\texttt{src/voxel\_mg.py:fcg} implements FP64 Polak--Ribière flexible
CG, including when the preconditioner computes its V-cycle in FP32.
Start with \texttt{x=0,\ r=b},
\texttt{\seqsplit{z=B(r),} \seqsplit{p=z,} \seqsplit{delta=r\textasciicircum{}T} \seqsplit{z}}.
Each iteration computes:

\begin{verbatim}
q = A p
alpha = delta / (p^T q)
x = x + alpha p
r_new = r - alpha q
if a requested true-residual check is due:
    r_new = b - A x
    stop if ||r_new|| / ||b|| <= tolerance
z_new = B(r_new)
beta = r_new^T (z_new-z) / delta
p = z_new + beta p
delta = r_new^T z_new
r, z = r_new, z_new
\end{verbatim}

The checked residual replaces the recursive residual. The routine
defaults to \texttt{check\_every=5}, but D/E/I frozen protocols pass
\texttt{check\_every=1}: a successful to-tolerance iteration therefore
costs two outer products (search direction and true residual).
Fixed-work mode skips intermediate replacement and uses K direction
products plus one final true-residual product, K+1 total. A capped
to-tolerance exit executes the final check as well: with
\texttt{check\_every=1}, a cap of K iterations costs \texttt{2K+1} outer
products if no early return succeeds. This recurrence describes the
rediscretized solver; G/H retain the separate donor solver and its
tolerance.

\subsection{Damping estimator and
limits}\label{damping-estimator-and-limits}

\texttt{spectral\_omegas} estimates the spectral radius of
constrained/free-space \texttt{D\^{}-1\ A} on each noncoarsest level. It
initializes a NumPy standard-normal vector, masks fixed DOFs and
normalizes it. For 30 iterations it computes \texttt{w=D\^{}-1\ A\ v},
masks fixed DOFs, sets
\texttt{lambda=\textbar{}\textbar{}w\textbar{}\textbar{}\_2} and
\texttt{v=w/lambda}. Then \texttt{omega=4/(3\ lambda)}. The random
generator is restarted on each level with the same seed, not advanced
continuously across levels. Default seed is 20260923; runners pass their
protocol \texttt{order\_seed} (D/E: 2026092320; I: 2026092621). The
reference FP64 hierarchy uses node-owned products; every arm shares its
estimated weights and the same host-computed coarsest factor, with that
factor cast to the arm's preconditioner precision on construction.

Thirty power steps are an estimate, not a certified upper eigenvalue
bound. Empirical evidence consists of CPU smoother/transfer tests and
the retained successful residual/energy checks for the tested
coefficient fields. A fixed 0.6 damping failed heterogeneous CPU cases.
Neither the estimator nor those tests establish universal smoother
stability or mixed-precision convergence. The exact rational
factorization certificates in the separate pinned-release audit concern
different ideal operators and must not be attributed to this power
estimate.

\subsection{Uncertainty and decisions}\label{uncertainty-and-decisions}

Session E computes a paired ratio for each \textbf{round}: numerator
time divided by candidate time. The statistic is the median of these
ratios, not the ratio of medians. Its one-sided 95\% lower bound is the
5th percentile of 10,000 bootstrap sample medians, resampling the round
ratios with replacement using NumPy
\texttt{\seqsplit{default\_rng(order\_seed)}} (\texttt{2026092320});
sample size equals the number of paired rounds. Twenty products within
one round are repeated work in a batch, not twenty independent
observations. See
\texttt{\seqsplit{experiments/session\_e/analysis.py:bootstrap\_lower}}
and the frozen protocol's H8/H9 rules. H8 requires median ≥1.05 and
lower bound ≥1.02 against the fastest declared donor on both primary
cases, with all rounds and checks present. H9 requires its separately
declared all-FP64 fixed-work thresholds. The synthetic study uses its
own bootstrap seed (\texttt{20260924}) and declared thresholds; A--D use
their frozen analysis/protocol rules.

A narrow interval such as 1.259 with lower bound 1.258 describes
within-session round variability conditional on the measured
host/configuration. It does not quantify uncertainty over rental hosts,
GPU populations, arbitrary designs or the choice of implementation. The
two-host E replication is a separate outcome, not a bootstrap population
sample.

G/H use \textbf{median-threshold decisions}, not one-sided bootstrap
tests. For each primary all-FP64 case on a host, H11/H11b requires
median paired wall ratio ≥1.10, at least two completed repetitions,
total Krylov counts matched within 3\% in every pair, and the
compliance/volume/solve gate. Replication requires both hosts to pass.
The generated paired table prints all three observations, including
variability and unfavorable results. Mixed-schedule H12/H12b comparisons
are descriptive medians with a declared expectation below 1.10; they are
not confidence-bound decisions. Single capped trajectories have no
repeated-sampling uncertainty estimate. Session I H13/H14 also use
declared median thresholds on three fixed-work rounds; two tolerance
times support a descriptive ordering among the tested routes, not a
statistical universal winner.

The donor log's final compliance is evaluated at the density

the retained endpoint conventions, not a new equilibrium solve on the
saved final density. Design change is the infinity norm of the
unfiltered design-variable update \texttt{max(abs(x\_new-x))}; the cap
criterion implemented in the frozen runner is

design has converged. These are capped end-to-end trajectories; no
time-to-converged-design claim is made.

final logged compliance to stock, using
\texttt{\seqsplit{abs(C\_arm-C\_stock)/abs(C\_stock)} \seqsplit{<=} \seqsplit{1e-3}}.

\texttt{1e-3} of the preset volume fraction and no solve whose iteration
count reaches the 1000-iteration cap. This is compliance/volume
agreement under that coverage; it is not density-field equivalence. The
current analysis does not compute a density-field norm, maximum per-step
compliance difference, or a same-state independent residual for every
optimization solve. Stored design arrays cover repetition 1 and the
capped trajectories; repetitions 2/3 have scalar histories, not stored
fields. The frozen protocol's intention to report field norms must not
be confused with completed verification. The exposed
\texttt{equivalence} compatibility field has precisely this narrower
meaning.

\subsection{Resident libCEED

The tested library is v0.12.0, commit
\texttt{\seqsplit{4018a20a98d451fac24765d3ddb936861647ce8d}}, archive
SHA-256
\texttt{\seqsplit{4c642ae1563e0363aa48f5d794832ad6e9716789405e33511213b837d1528a23}}.
\texttt{\seqsplit{evidence/companion/results/rescope/hex-modal-libceed-resident-20260924/protocol.json}}

The extracted bridge and QFunction are retained as
\texttt{\seqsplit{provenance/supplement/libceed-bridge.c}} and
\texttt{libceed-elasticity.h}, with archive/member hashes in
\texttt{\seqsplit{provenance/supplement/extractions.json}}. The bridge
creates a 3D, three-component Q1 basis with two Gauss points per axis. A
discontinuous constant scalar basis stores one density per cell; the
QFunction evaluates \texttt{E=1e-6+(1-1e-6)rho\^{}3}, with unit modulus,
Poisson ratio 0.3 and exact unit-cube geometry scaling. It does not
store eight replicated density coefficients per cell. Library nodes are
\texttt{(z,y,x,component)} while the custom padded layout is
\texttt{(x,z,y,component)}; a reflected-y restriction maps the same
physical nodes. Constraints are exact identity rows imposed by a GPU
kernel after the library action.

\texttt{/gpu/cuda/shared} and \texttt{/gpu/cuda/gen}, with device-memory
preference checked. One action includes output clearing/assembly, the
density-scaled elastic law, identity rows and
\texttt{\seqsplit{cudaDeviceSynchronize}}. Host steady-clock batched
time is primary; CUDA-event time is secondary. Setup, conversion,
allocation, transfers, compilation, JIT warmup and numerical output are
excluded. Input/material vectors remain resident. There are 12
randomized blocks of 256 calls, five seconds of warmup, and a minimum
five-millisecond batch criterion. Six primary cases are 64×32×32,
128×64×64 and 192×96×96, each with two coefficient patterns; 18 total
shapes/patterns and separate sanitizer runs receive correctness checks.

The 2.5768--3.0083 ratio is generated-backend time divided by
signed-multiply-add time for those six cases
(\texttt{figures/ratios.csv}; regenerate via
\texttt{\seqsplit{analysis/session\_e\_tables.py}}). It comes from one
RTX 4090 allocation and a different harness from the main CuPy study. It
does not establish superiority over libCEED as a whole, other backend
choices, or tensor implementations in general. The declared
sign-test/Holm rule covers all 30 case/control comparisons and must not
be replaced with Session E's paired-bootstrap rule.

denominator, normalized bilinear defect and absolute correction leakage,
and reproduces their selected maxima from hash-verified saved arrays.

percentages. In particular, the magnitude of absolute fixed-DOF leakage
scales with the residual input; preservation of the free space is the
violated invariant.

The \href{hypothesis-registry.md}{registry} distinguishes named
hypotheses from descriptive expectations, packaging failures,
integration gates and operational attempts. H3 was not confirmed, H5 was
not supported, and H11/H11b/H15 failed their recorded decisions. H11b
concerns a changed pipeline; it does not erase H11. No global count of
three failures is claimed.

counter permission and tooling) remain separate attempts, including
Session J's seven attempts. Each final protocol is linked to matching
execution records; earlier failed attempts use their own amendments.

outcomes remain preserved. New adaptations are explicitly labeled,
including the boundary projection, corrected OC and GPU optimizer.
